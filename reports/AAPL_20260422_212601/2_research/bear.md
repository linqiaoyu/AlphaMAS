The "ecosystem lock-in" is real, but it's also seeing unprecedented regulatory assault that you dismiss too lightly.

Because the practical concerns—a **$3,499 price tag**, limited battery life, and unclear "killer apps"—started to sink in. Pouring $30 billion in R&D (a 14% increase) to chase a niche market while your core cash cows stall is not a bullish signal; it's a sign of a company struggling to find its next act, throwing capital at the wall to see what sticks.

**Let's address your bullet points directly:**
*   **"Valuation is too rich."** You say "you pay a premium for quality." But you're paying a growth premium for a company that just posted a **year of negative revenue growth**. The pristine balance sheet is admirable, but it's a defensive attribute, not a growth catalyst. Government bans on Apple devices among state employees in China are a real and growing risk. Supply chain diversification is costly and dilutive to margins.

**Reflecting on past lessons:** You're right—bears have underestimated the ecosystem. My lesson learned is not to bet against the installed base. But the new lesson forming is: **you can overpay for that ecosystem, especially when its growth rate is converging with that of a utility, and its moat is being legislatively bridged.** The bull's mistake now is extrapolating past ecosystem wins onto entirely new, unproven, and regulatory-targeted battlegrounds.
